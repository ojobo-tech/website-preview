AI is not simply another tool to add to existing work. Its promise depends on understanding the problem, redesigning the process, and building the skills and culture needed to use it well.

A career in three parts

My work has moved through analytics and business intelligence, data science and applied research, and AI transformation. Across those phases, the common thread has been turning complex information into useful decisions and useful systems.

That has meant working across education, finance, healthcare, public-interest research, and community development. The technical build matters, but so does the organizational setting in which it must create value.

How I work

I begin with the outcome: what needs to be solved, achieved, or improved? From there I examine the process, identify a specific use case, consider the capability people need, choose appropriate technology, and attend to the culture that supports experimentation and sustained change.

Sometimes the right contribution is a framework or a conversation. Sometimes it is a platform, a data product, or an applied research effort. I am interested in both the thinking and the making.

Finding your way around

The transformation framework explains how I think. The technical portfolio shows what I build. Research and writing develop the ideas, while the reading room gathers some of what is shaping my perspective.

If one of those threads connects with a problem you are working on, let's talk.
